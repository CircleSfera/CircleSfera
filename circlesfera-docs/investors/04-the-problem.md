## The market fails the five principles

CircleSfera is built on five product principles: user control comes first; algorithmic transparency; no hidden suppression; strict and explicit moderation; responsible data handling. The incumbent social market systematically violates them. That is not an accident. It is how those products grew.

---

## User control was traded for engagement

The user was supposed to decide what they consume and who interacts with them. Instead, automated systems override explicit choices. The feed optimizes for time spent, not for what the person asked to see. Muting, hiding, and keyword filters exist on some surfaces — often as afterthoughts — while the default experience remains opaque.

CircleSfera's response: feed hide, mute author, mute keyword, and privacy settings are persisted domain data, not client-side filters. The user reshapes the home feed; the system applies it.

---

## Algorithmic transparency became a black box

Recommendation and ranking use many signals. On most platforms, the user cannot reasonably answer: why this post, why this profile, which of my actions changes what I see next? Signals became proprietary and arbitrary. Trust eroded.

Creators professionalized inside that black box. They guess at reach. They buy tools to analyse what the platform will not explain.

---

## Hidden suppression became a management tool

Shadow banning — reducing visibility without telling the account holder — is widely used as an ecosystem management tool. Content visibility no longer follows explicit, verifiable criteria. Decisions affecting distribution are kept hidden. User trust requires the opposite.

CircleSfera does not adopt hidden or arbitrary visibility reduction. When content or an account is limited, the grounds must be objective, communicable, and traceable.

---

## Moderation lives in policy documents, not in the product

When something is limited, users often receive no clear account of what happened, why, under which rule, or how to contest it. Moderation actions are difficult to audit from the outside. Appeals, when they exist, feel like a form letter.

Trust increases when moderation is understandable and justifiable. It decreases when it is arbitrary or opaque.

---

## Identity and money are fragmented

The digital life of a creator is split across applications: publish here, talk there, cash out somewhere else. A paid badge is sold as proof of identity when it is only proof of payment. KYC and subscription tier are conflated. The fan pays on a different domain than where they discovered the creator.

Incumbents proved they can connect billions of people. They were designed in another period and adapted by addition. Checkout was added to a feed, or a feed was added to a fan site. Neither is a single system.

---

## Data handling outran justification

Platforms collect more than they can explain. Purpose, proportionality, minimization, and retention are legal requirements — and product requirements. Users retain rights to their content and personal data. Uses that significantly affect experience must remain explainable.

---

## The structural gap

Connecting people is no longer the whole job. A platform must balance experience, trust, creation, monetization, and ecosystem health.

The gap CircleSfera addresses: communication, content, identity, money, and trust belong in one experience — designed that way from the beginning, not bolted on after a decade of the opposite promise.
